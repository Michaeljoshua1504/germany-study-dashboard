✈️ Visa & Relocation Journey — Admission to Arrival
Every step from the moment you accept an offer to settling into life in Germany, broken into phases. Tick items off as you go — your progress is saved in this browser.

Phase 0 — Before You Apply (mostly done) ▶
Foundational documents every Indian applicant needs before a German university or the embassy will even look at your file.
Phase 1 — After Receiving an Admission Letter (Zulassungsbescheid) ▶
The clock starts here. This letter is the foundation document everything else depends on.
Phase 2 — Financial Proof: Blocked Account (Sperrkonto) ▶
2026 requirement: €11,904 (€992 × 12 months) deposited up front, released at €992/month after you arrive. The money is "blocked" — you cannot touch it until you land in Germany. Open this 6–8 weeks before your visa appointment. ✅ Chosen provider: Expatrio Value Package (expatrio.com) — €119 setup fee + €9/month. Accepted by all German authorities.
Phase 3 — Health Insurance ▶
Health insurance is mandatory for your visa and university enrollment. The Expatrio Value Package bundles two layers of coverage automatically. ✅ Chosen provider: TK (Techniker Krankenkasse) — Germany's largest public insurer, English-language support, fully digital, accepted by all universities.
Phase 4 — Student Visa Application (VFS Bangalore → Consulate Bengaluru) ▶
One-shot process — note that the remonstration (appeal) procedure for visa rejections was abolished from July 2025, so the file needs to be complete and correct the first time.
Phase 5 — Pre-Departure Preparation ▶
Once the visa is in hand, the focus shifts to logistics.
Phase 6 — First 2 Weeks in Germany (legally time-critical) ▶
Germany has hard legal deadlines here — don't let these slip. Enrollment is already done online — focus shifts to legal registration and getting your student access set up.
Phase 7 — Work Rights & Post-Study Stay (good to know) ▶
You are allowed to work while studying and stay in Germany after graduation to find a job. These are not urgent actions but important to know ahead of time.

Goal: A1-level German by 31 July 2026, taught live — not self-study. Each day follows the same 4-hour structure below.
📅 How each day works (3–4 hrs): Hour 1 — New Lesson (grammar + vocab explained) → Hour 2 — Practice & Writing (exercises, corrected live) → Hour 3 — Voice Chat (speaking + listening practice) → Hour 4 (some days) — Review & Consolidation. A day is only marked complete after all three core hours are done.
